Let Search Data Pick Your Next Web Page: A Local Business Example, by Danielle Lemons

At DirecTech Connect in Nashville, we build websites and run SEO for small local businesses. The most useful planning tool we have is free: Google Search Console. It shows the searches a site already appears for, including the ones it has no real page for. Here is one example from a landscaping company north of Nashville.

What the data showed

In June 2026, Search Console showed the site appearing for 143 different searches, including very local searches from nearby towns. It also showed real demand for services the site had no page for, like bushhogging and retaining walls. The demand was there; the site just wasn't capturing it.

What we changed

  • In July we added dedicated pages for those two services, written answer-first so each page opens with what the customer asked.
  • We added FAQ structured data to all 14 of the site's questions and answers.
  • We kept the Google Business Profile posting on a steady twice-a-week schedule.

The results

In July, 63 of the site's 64 visitors were brand new, and July alone brought in more new people than all of June. Comparing the first 90 days of tracking with the last 90 days:

  • Clicks from Google went from 19 to 67.
  • Impressions went from 1,960 to 7,600.
  • Average position improved from 28.7 to 17.5.

The lesson

Don't guess what to build next. Search Console tells you what people are already looking for. When you see searches you show up for but have no real page for, that's your next page.
