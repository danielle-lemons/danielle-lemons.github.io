A One-Page Incident Response Plan for Small Businesses, by Danielle Lemons

At DirecTech Connect in Nashville, we handle phones, internet and IT for small businesses. When something goes wrong, the first hour decides how bad it gets, and most small offices spend that hour figuring out who's in charge. A plan that fits on one page fixes that.

The five questions your plan has to answer

  1. Who gets told first, and how? Name the person, and give a phone number, not just an email address.
  2. Which devices get disconnected? Unplug the affected computer from the network. Don't turn it off until your IT provider says so.
  3. Which passwords get changed, and from which device? Use a computer or phone you know is clean, not the one that may be compromised.
  4. Who calls the bank? Use the number on the back of the card, never a number from an email or text.
  5. Where is the list? Keep a current list of every computer, account and person with access, so you know what to lock down.

Put a name next to each step, so nobody spends the first hour deciding who's in charge.

Make reporting safe

The employee who clicks a bad link and speaks up in two minutes isn't the problem. The one who stays quiet gives the attacker time. Tell your team, in writing, that reporting a mistake fast will never get them in trouble.

Print it

Print the page with phone numbers for your IT provider, your bank and your key staff, and keep it somewhere other than your computers. If email or your network is what's under attack, you can't count on it to coordinate the response. During the recent Microsoft outage, calls to my business still reached my cell through our phone system's app, and that separate channel kept us working.
