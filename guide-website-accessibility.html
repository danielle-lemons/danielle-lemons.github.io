Website Accessibility for Small Businesses, by Danielle Lemons

At DirecTech Connect we build, host and run SEO for small business websites, and accessibility is part of every site review we do. Most of the fixes are simple, and they help every visitor, not only people with a disability or older adults.

The problems we see most

  • Small, light-gray text on a white background.
  • Phone numbers shown as images or plain text that can't be tapped to call.
  • Photos and buttons with no alt text.
  • The main action, like "Schedule a visit" or "Get a quote," buried behind a menu.

For a visitor who is already in a hurry or worried, every extra step is a reason to close the tab.

Make it easy to read and move around

Use body text at 18px or larger with strong contrast. Keep the menu short, with plain labels like "Pricing," "Services" and "Contact." Make buttons big, with space around them, and put the phone number at the top of every page as a tap-to-call link. Keep one main action per page.

Video, galleries and anything interactive

Caption every video and never autoplay sound. Give galleries and tours keyboard controls and a written description of what's shown, and make sure everything still works when someone zooms the page to 200%.

Does it hurt conversions?

Usually it's the opposite. Accessibility work mostly removes friction: bigger buttons, clearer labels, shorter forms. What hurts conversions is hiding the phone number or making a form ask too much.

What to measure

Track calls from tap-to-call links, form completions and booking requests in Google Analytics 4 as key events, and check the accessibility score in Google Lighthouse. Compare the 30 days before and after each change.

The standard to aim for

WCAG 2.2 Level AA is the practical target. Build a quick check into every update: run Lighthouse, add alt text to every new photo and test the page on a phone.

Top five fixes

  1. Put the phone number and your main action on every page.
  2. Raise text size and contrast.
  3. Caption your videos.
  4. Keep forms short: name, phone and what you need.
  5. Give every photo alt text that says what it shows. Search engines read alt text too, so the same fix helps your visibility.
