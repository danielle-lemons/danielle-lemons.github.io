When a Big Cloud Service Goes Down: A Small-Business Outage Plan, by Danielle Lemons

At DirecTech Connect in Nashville, we sell business internet, phone service and IT support to small businesses. The recent Microsoft outage hit my company and our clients directly, so this is what it looked like from the ground, and what I'd tell any small business to set up before the next one.

How worried should you be?

Worried enough to plan, not to panic. The real lesson of a big outage is how many separate services quietly depend on the same few providers. When one of them goes down, email, file sharing, sign-ins and even phone systems can fail together, because they were all built on the same foundation.

What kept us working

During the Microsoft outage, calls to my business still reached my cell through our phone system's mobile app. That separate channel is the whole point. Customers could still reach us while email was unreliable, and we could tell them what was happening.

For our clients, we pair fiber internet with a 5G backup line so calls and payments keep working if the main line goes down. A second path costs far less than a day of missed calls.

Four steps for a small business

  1. List which tools depend on which provider: email, files, sign-ins, phones, payments.
  2. Make sure at least one way to reach customers and staff doesn't share that dependency, such as a phone app that rings your cell.
  3. Add a backup internet line if your phones and card machines run over the internet.
  4. Print a one-page plan with phone numbers for your IT provider, your bank and key staff. You can't count on email to coordinate a response when email is what's down.

For customers and consumers

Keep a little cash on hand, know your bank's phone number (it's on the back of your card), and never sign in through a link someone texts or emails you during an outage. Scammers count on the confusion.
