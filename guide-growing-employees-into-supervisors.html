Growing Employees Into Supervisors: Three Levels of Delegation, by Danielle Lemons

Small business owners often hire supervisors from outside because nobody inside seems ready. Usually the real problem is that every decision still waits on the owner, so nobody gets the practice. Supervisors are grown one decision at a time.

Sort decisions into three levels

  • Decide alone. Routine, low-cost and easy to correct, like rescheduling an appointment or reordering standard supplies under a set dollar amount.
  • Decide and report. The employee makes the call, then tells the manager what they decided and why. A refund up to a set amount or a small policy exception can fit here.
  • Recommend for approval. For larger costs or longer consequences, the employee gathers the facts, proposes an answer and waits for a yes. The difference from simply asking is that they bring a recommendation, not just a question.

Write the levels down

A one-page list naming each common decision, its level and any dollar limits lets a new employee learn it in an afternoon. A long-time employee no longer has to guess where the line sits.

Move decisions down as people earn it

A new hire starts with more decisions at the recommend level. As they show good judgment, those decisions move down a level. The list becomes a visible career path for both sides.

Back the decisions people make

If someone makes a reasonable call within their level and it turns out differently than you would have chosen, talk it through privately and adjust the list. Overruling people in front of customers teaches them to stop deciding.

Review the list every few months

If the same recommend-level question comes up every week and the answer is always yes, it belongs one level down. If a decide-alone call caused a problem, move it up and explain why. That review is the mentoring conversation.

Start this week

List ten decisions that landed on your desk this month, assign each one a level and share the list with your team.
