Business Internet and Backup Connections for Small Offices, by Danielle Lemons

For most small offices, the internet connection now carries the phones, the email, the card reader and every shared file. When it goes down, the whole business stops. At DirecTech Connect we sell business internet, 5G internet and cloud phone service, so a big part of my job is checking what a location can really deliver before a client commits to it.

Check the exact address before you sign anything

Service is decided address by address, not city by city. Two buildings on the same street can have very different options. Before signing a lease or a contract, ask a provider to check that exact address and suite for fiber, cable and 5G. If the building needs new construction to bring fiber in, ask how long that takes, because it can mean weeks without the connection you planned on.

Look at upload speed, not just download

Advertised speeds usually lead with download. Cloud phones, video calls and file backups also depend on upload. Symmetrical fiber, where upload matches download, makes a noticeable difference for an office that runs its phones in the cloud. Start from how many people work at the same time and what they do all day, then pick the tier.

Plan for the day the main line goes down

Every connection fails sometimes: a cut cable, a storm, a carrier outage. A second path keeps the business running. A common setup is fiber as the main line with a 5G connection as the backup, on a router that switches over on its own. If you can, put the backup on a different carrier from the main line, so one outage does not take out both.

Keep calls coming in during an outage

If your phones run in the cloud, set up forwarding rules before you need them, so calls ring to cell phones when the office connection is down. A phone system mobile app helps here too. During a Microsoft outage that hit us and our clients, calls kept working because people could answer on the phone system's mobile app.

Plan the cutover

Switching internet providers touches everything. Schedule the change for a slow time, keep the old service active until the new one is tested, and write down the router settings, static IP addresses and anything else that points at the old connection. Check the phones, the card reader, the printers and any cameras after the switch, not just the computers.

Questions to ask a provider

  • What service is available at this exact address and suite, and when can it be installed?
  • What are the upload and download speeds, and are they the same?
  • Is there a backup option, and does it switch over automatically?
  • Who do we call when it goes down, and how fast do they respond?
  • What does the contract say about moving, upgrading or canceling?
